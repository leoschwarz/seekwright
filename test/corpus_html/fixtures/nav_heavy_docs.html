Section 12 — Mooring lines

A mooring line is only as good as the splice that ends it, and the manual insists on a long splice because a short one hardens where it bends.

Line diameters are set by the length of the vessel and by the height of the quay, not by the weather of the day, which is why a list of diameters belongs in a printed chapter rather than in a sign on the wall.

The chapter closes with the three knots a deckhand is expected to tie with one hand, in the order the examiner asks for them.
